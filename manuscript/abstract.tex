\begin{abstract}
On November 20, 2026, Levent Alp\"oge announced a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, found in collaboration with Claude and released as a Lean development. The Lean and Mathlib communities subsequently certified the formal statement and proof.

A quantitative form of Landau's theorem on Mellin transforms reduces the zero-free half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s>1/2$ for Dirichlet $L$-functions to a one-sided square-root bound for smoothed prime sums on suitable intervals. Alp\"oge adapts Stepanov's point-counting method over finite fields. Divisibility gained at inert primes through successive theta lifts plays the role of vanishing to high order at rational points. Each factor of an inert prime $p$ contributes $\log p$ to the divisibility term in the slope inequality. For a sum of length $X$, a chain of about $\sqrt X$ theta lifts balances the errors in that inequality and gives the one-sided square-root bound required by Landau's criterion.

This reconstruction is written so that mathematicians can referee the argument from beginning to end using the paper and its references, without consulting Lean. Each remaining hypothesis is stated where the deduction requires it.
\end{abstract}
