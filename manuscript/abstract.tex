\begin{abstract}
On November 20, 2026, Levent Alp\"oge announced a proof of the Riemann hypothesis that he and Claude had found together. He released the proof in Lean. The Lean and Mathlib communities subsequently certified the formal statement and proof.

A quantitative form of Landau's theorem on Mellin transforms reduces the zero-free half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s>1/2$ for Dirichlet $L$-functions to a one-sided square-root bound for smoothed prime sums on suitable intervals. Alp\"oge adapts Stepanov's point-counting method over finite fields. The divisibility that successive theta lifts gain at inert primes plays the role of vanishing to high order at rational points. Each factor of an inert prime $p$ contributes $\log p$ to the divisibility term in the slope inequality. For a sum of length $X$, a chain of about $\sqrt X$ theta lifts balances the errors in that inequality and gives the one-sided square-root bound that Landau's criterion requires.

We wrote this so that mathematicians can follow the argument from beginning to end and check it for themselves without consulting Lean. In the course of the reconstruction, we found what appears to be a shorter proof of a uniform bound in Galois cohomology (\S\ref{tw:effectiveK}). That shortcut gives us reason to think the proof as a whole may admit a simpler argument.
\end{abstract}
