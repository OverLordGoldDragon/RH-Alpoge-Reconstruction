\begin{abstract}
On November 20, 2026, Levent Alp\"oge announced a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, found in collaboration with Claude and released as a Lean development. The Lean and Mathlib communities subsequently certified the formal statement and proof. This manuscript reconstructs the argument in conventional mathematics.

A quantitative form of Landau's theorem on Mellin transforms reduces the zero-free half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s>1/2$ for Dirichlet $L$-functions to a one-sided square-root bound for smoothed prime sums on suitable intervals. To obtain this bound, Alp\"oge adapts Stepanov's method for counting points on curves over finite fields. Where an auxiliary function vanishes repeatedly at each rational point, successive theta lifts accumulate divisibility at each inert prime $p$. Weighting these exponents by $\log p$ gives the prime sum in a slope inequality. For sums of length $X$, a chain of about $\sqrt X$ lifts balances the errors.

The reconstruction is not yet complete. Section~\ref{sec:frontier} states the remaining base-change, local realization, counting and cohomological estimates, together with the hypotheses still used in the paper. The goal is a human-readable proof that can be checked independently of the Lean development.
\end{abstract}
