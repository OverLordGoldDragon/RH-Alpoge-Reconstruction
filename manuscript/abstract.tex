\begin{abstract}
On November 20, 2026, Levent Alp\"oge announced a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, found in collaboration with Claude and released as a Lean development. The Lean and Mathlib communities subsequently certified the formal statement and proof. This manuscript reconstructs the argument in conventional mathematics.

Stepanov's method for counting points on a curve over a finite field uses a rational function that vanishes to order at least $m$ at each rational point. Its pole degree then bounds $m$ times the number of points. In Alp\"oge's argument, a chain of $m$ theta lifts plays the role of this multiplicity. At each inert prime $p$, the lifts accumulate $p$-adic divisibility. Weighting these exponents by $\log p$ turns a slope inequality into an upper bound for a prime sum. For sums of length $X$, taking $m$ about $\sqrt X$ balances the errors and gives the estimate needed to exclude zeros to the right of the critical line.

The reconstruction is not yet complete. Section~\ref{sec:frontier} states the remaining base-change, local realization, counting and cohomological estimates, together with the hypotheses still used in the paper. The goal is a human-readable proof that can be checked independently of the Lean development.
\end{abstract}
